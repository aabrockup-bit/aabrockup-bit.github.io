Officials Clock — Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 27, 2026

Officials Clock is a game clock, scoring, and notes app for football officials. It has a Wear OS watch app and a companion Android phone app. It is published on Google Play by the developer Officials Clock ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the app collects, where your information goes, and how to delete it.

The short version

  • Your account is just your email address and password, kept by Google's Firebase Authentication. We never see your password.
  • Your games stay with you. Scores, clocks, the game log, coin toss results, voice notes, and game reports are kept on your own watch and phone. The app never uploads them to us or to any server of ours. We don't run any servers.
  • Voice uses your device's speech recognition. The app asks for on-device recognition first. If your device can't do that, its built-in speech service (usually Google's) handles the request and may process the audio online. See "Voice and microphone" below.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking, and we never sell or share your data.

Voice and microphone

  • The microphone turns on only for a voice command or a voice note. You start it by tapping the mic, Record, or "Say name" button, or by pressing a spare side button on watches that have one. After a command that needs a yes or no, the mic reopens briefly to hear your answer.
  • There is no always-on listening and no wake word.
  • Voice commands and voice-note transcripts use the speech recognizer that comes with your watch or phone. Speech is recognized on the device when on-device recognition is available and the language pack is installed. That takes Android 12 or newer for voice commands, and Android 13 or newer for voice-note transcripts. Otherwise the device's default speech service, usually Google's, recognizes it and may send the audio to its servers. That processing is covered by that service's privacy policy, not ours.
  • On older watches, voice notes are recorded but not transcribed.
  • The app never sends your audio to us or to any server of ours. Voice-note recordings go only to your own phone, as described in "How the watch and phone connect."
  • The watch keeps a small "voice misses" list so the voice commands can be improved. It includes commands it couldn't understand, commands you answered "no" to or corrected, and the words it heard for each. The list is saved with each watch game's report. It stays on your devices unless you share it.

What stays on your devices

  • Game data: team names, scores, clocks, timeouts, downs, the game log, coin toss results, and settings. This includes games you run on the phone in "This phone" mode.
  • Voice notes: recordings and their transcripts. They can describe players and incidents, such as an ejection, so the app keeps them in private storage that other apps can't read.
  • Game reports: a PDF of each game, including voice-note transcripts, plus the voice-misses list. For "This phone" games, the phone makes only the PDF. That game's voice misses stay in the phone app's private storage until you start a new game.
  • None of this is included in phone backups or device-to-device transfers.

How the watch and phone connect

The watch and phone apps talk to each other through Google's Wear OS connection (the Wearable Data Layer). It works only between this app on your own paired devices.

  • The phone sends settings, team names, and your account email address to the watch, so the watch knows you're signed in. The watch sends its settings and team names back.
  • While the phone's Scoreboard is open, and for up to 15 seconds after, the watch sends team names, scores, clocks, timeouts, and the latest game-log lines to the phone. The Scoreboard's buttons can control the game on the watch.
  • When a game ends (including after overtime), or when you tap Send report to phone or say "send report to phone," the watch sends that game's report, its voice-note recordings, and the voice-misses list to your phone.

When the devices are near each other, all of this goes over Bluetooth or local Wi-Fi. When they're apart, Wear OS can relay any of it, including your email address, encrypted through Google's servers. The watch app has no internet access of its own.

Sharing

We don't sell, rent, or share personal data. A report or note leaves the app only when you choose to share or open it. Share sends it to the app you pick. Opening a report, note, or voice-misses file, from the Reports tab or from the report notification, lets the viewer or player app that opens it read that one file.

Security

  • Passwords are hashed by Google. Google rate-limits sign-in attempts, and the app adds a 30-second pause after five failed tries.
  • New accounts must confirm their email address before the app unlocks.
  • Password-reset links expire and work only once.
  • Deleting your account in the app requires your password.
  • Account traffic is encrypted with HTTPS, and the apps block unencrypted connections.

Deleting your data

  • Your account, in the app: after you've confirmed your email, go to phone app → Account tab → Delete my account, then enter your password. This permanently deletes your account and email address from Firebase and signs your watch out. It doesn't delete the games, notes, or reports on your watch and phone. Remove those as described below.
  • Your account, by email: email officialsclock.app@gmail.com from the address on your account, with the subject "Delete my Officials Clock account". This also works if you never confirmed your email address. We'll delete the account within 30 days and confirm by email. This doesn't remove the copies of your email address saved on your phone and watch. To remove those, sign out in the phone app (Account tab → Sign out), or clear the app's data or uninstall it.
  • Voice notes: on the watch, hold a note to delete it. This removes the recording and its transcript from the current game on the watch only. Copies already sent to your phone, and transcripts inside reports already saved on the watch or phone, stay until you delete those files. After you start a new game, the watch deletes the previous game's recordings the next time the app closes. That game's report, with the note transcripts, stays on the watch.
  • Reports on the phone: Reports tab → Delete removes a report, voice note, or voice-misses file.
  • Everything on a device: uninstall the app or clear its data. The watch keeps only its 40 newest report files and deletes older ones automatically. Nothing from your games is stored anywhere except your own watch and phone.

Children

Officials Clock is made for adult sports officials. It isn't directed to children under 13.
